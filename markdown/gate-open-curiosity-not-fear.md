Today, I had a small panic moment.

I was taking my pups out for grooming and accidentally left the gate open. All of them ran out. I was chasing them, heart racing, scared a car might hit them or worse, that they might jump on a kid on the road and I’d have to deal with the consequences.

Eventually, I caught up and called them. Most of them ran back to me.

Except one.

He ran straight toward a kid nearby.
I froze.

This is the same pup who jumps on me and barks at guests who come home. I was sure he’d scare the kid.

But to my surprise, he was calm. No barking. No jumping. Just quietly standing next to the kids, letting them pet him.

That moment really made me think.

Why is it that my pups bark and pounce when guests come home, but stay calm with kids on the street?

Maybe it’s fear.

Most guests are already scared. They pull back, tense up, and see my pups as animals that might hurt them. The kids, on the other hand, had no fear. They just wanted to love them. No judgment. No threat.

And maybe my pups felt that.

It made me wonder, how often do we react to people not because of who they are, but because of what we think they might do? How much calmer would our interactions be if we approached people with curiosity instead of fear?
